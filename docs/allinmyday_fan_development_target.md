# ALLINMYDAY Designer Fan — Development Target

## Product target
- 1200 mm / 48 in ceiling fan
- BLDC architecture
- Original Allinmyday industrial design
- High airflow with low electrical input
- Easy maintenance and modular service parts
- Target factory/BOM cost: **below ₹3,000** at volume; prototype cost may be higher
- Target retail: **₹3,200–₹4,500**, subject to GST, channel margin, warranty, certification, packaging and actual BOM

## Current market reality
The Indian 1200 mm BLDC market already includes products below ₹3,000, including examples around ₹2,399–₹2,849. Therefore, Allinmyday must compete on a differentiated design, serviceability, airflow/noise balance, warranty and/or smart features—not simply BLDC efficiency.

## Cost-down architecture
1. Use a proven bulk BLDC motor/driver platform for the first product revision.
2. Avoid custom magnetic-energy hardware in the low-cost launch model unless testing proves a clear benefit.
3. Use injection-moulded/pressure-die-cast parts where tooling economics support volume.
4. Keep the blade architecture simple: three aerodynamic blades for the baseline cost model; test four blades only if airflow/noise data justifies the added parts.
5. Use a common controller across multiple fan models.
6. Minimize unique fasteners and decorative parts.
7. Design for screw access and modular replacement of motor/controller/blade arms.
8. Keep final QC, balancing and firmware/configuration under controlled Allinmyday procedures.

## Engineering targets — NOT verified claims
- Power: 25–35 W target class
- Air delivery: 210–260 CMM target range
- Speed: approximately 330–380 RPM depending on blade load and controller
- Noise: target below 35 dB under defined test conditions
- Product mass: approximately 4–5.5 kg target

These values must be measured on prototypes before being used in marketing, certification submissions or patent claims.

## Development phases
### Phase 1 — Home prototype
Existing fan baseline -> BLDC conversion -> measure watts/RPM/airflow/noise/temperature/vibration.

### Phase 2 — Blade optimization
Test multiple original blade geometries using CFD and physical measurements. Select the design with the best airflow/service-value/efficiency/noise balance.

### Phase 3 — Cost-down prototype
Replace temporary parts with production-intent motor, housing, blades, controller and mounting hardware. Build a detailed should-cost BOM.

### Phase 4 — Compliance prototype
Freeze mechanical/electrical revision and perform the applicable safety/performance/energy tests before commercial claims.

### Phase 5 — Production
Multi-vendor sourcing, incoming inspection, controlled assembly, dynamic balancing, final QC and traceability.
