# iAmYansi ARC-X — Everyday-Material Energy Concept

## Design goal
Create an original Arc-Core-shaped energy-conversion platform using inexpensive, commonly available materials and multiple real energy-conversion mechanisms. The device must not claim perpetual/free energy. Every watt must have a measurable source.

## Core concept: Energy-Cascade Toroid

The visual Arc Core is a toroidal housing containing independent generator tiles. The tiles feed a common DC energy bus. The bus can charge a safe storage buffer and power a load. No single exotic material is assumed to be the source of energy.

### Generator tiles
1. **Solar tile** — ordinary photovoltaic material; captures sunlight.
2. **Thermal tile** — thermoelectric material between a hot-side aluminium/copper spreader and a cool-side finned heat sink; captures useful temperature differences.
3. **Vibration tile** — copper coil + permanent magnet + spring/flexure; converts mechanical vibration to electricity.
4. **Triboelectric tile** — everyday polymer/paper/plastic surfaces with copper/aluminium electrodes; captures intermittent mechanical contact/separation energy. This is supported by recent research using waste plastic, ordinary paper and recovered copper.
5. **RF/ambient tile** — antenna + rectifier for very small ambient RF energy; treated as supplemental only.

## Everyday material candidates
- Aluminium: housing, heat spreader, fins.
- Copper: wire, coils, electrodes, busbars.
- Steel: springs/flexures and structural parts.
- Ferrite: magnetic/EM components where appropriate.
- Neodymium permanent magnets: vibration generator only, commercially sourced.
- PET/PTFE/Kapton-like films: triboelectric surfaces.
- Paper/cellulose: low-cost triboelectric surface candidate.
- Graphite/carbon: conductive/thermal experimental layers.
- Silicone/engineering polymer: insulation and mechanical isolation.
- Ceramic: electrical insulation and thermal stability where required.
- Bismuth-telluride-class commercial TEG modules: thermal harvesting prototype.
- Commercial photovoltaic cells: solar harvesting.

## Storage
The Arc Core itself is primarily a generator/converter. A secondary buffer may use a commercial supercapacitor and/or certified LFP battery pack. Storage capacity must be sized from measured harvested energy, not assumed energy creation.

## Key scientific rule
Magnets, coils, resonant circuits, capacitors, phase-change materials and common metals do not create net energy by themselves. They can convert, move, buffer or store energy. A closed loop that claims more output than all measured inputs requires extraordinary experimental evidence and must not be treated as established physics.

## Research direction
The novelty target is the **architecture and integration**: concentric generator tiles, shared energy bus, adaptive source selection, thermal routing, modular scaling, and Yansi-controlled measurement/optimization. This is deliberately different from a diamond/radioisotope battery concept.

## Scale
- Micro: sensor/IoT scale.
- Small: wearable/Holo-interface scale.
- Medium: desktop/portable power scale.
- Large: only after measured generation and thermal performance justify scaling.

House-scale power is not assumed from ambient harvesters. Current research demonstrates hybrid PV/thermal/vibration harvesters primarily for low-power electronics, while solar remains the practical high-power renewable input for homes.

## Safety architecture
Use independent hardware protections: over-current, over-voltage, over-temperature, insulation/fault monitoring, current limiting, contactor/isolation, fusing, and a physical emergency disconnect. Yansi can monitor and advise, but must never be the sole safety mechanism.

## Verification plan
For every generator tile measure:
- input energy source
- open-circuit voltage
- loaded voltage/current
- power
- efficiency where input power can be measured
- temperature
- duty cycle
- storage energy gained

The first prototype is a measurement platform, not a perpetual-energy claim.
